FlyCheap (https://flycheap.18dn.com) is a flight-price tracking and fare-alert
platform. Users set a specific route and a target price; our engine polls fares
continuously and alerts them by email and messaging channels when the fare drops
to their threshold or hits a statistically significant low against its own price
history.

Promotional method: we are a price-alert and content publisher, not a coupon,
loyalty, or incentive site. We place no toolbars, extensions, or adware. Outbound
traffic reaches partners through a server-side redirect that we own and log, and
we run no paid search on advertiser trademarks.

Why our traffic converts in-session: our users click at the moment they have been
told a specific fare has hit a specific price. They are not browsing — they are
acting on an alert about a trip they already decided to take. This matches
Booking.com's in-session attribution model, and it is why we are seeking a stays
partnership: a traveler who has just committed to a flight needs accommodation
for those exact dates, and we surface that at the point of decision.

Stage and scale, stated plainly: FlyCheap has been running in production since
May 2026. We have recorded over 64,000 fare observations across tracked routes
and driven roughly 3,900 outbound partner clicks to date, currently around 640
per 30 days. Our registered user base is small and early — we are a founder-
operated platform in its first year, growing deliberately rather than claiming
scale we do not have.

Audience: budget-conscious leisure travelers, primarily US and Canada, with
additional reach in Europe and the Middle East. The product ships in 8 languages
and is mobile-first.

Monetization: subscription tiers (free, $9/mo, $39/mo) plus travel affiliate
partnerships. We currently work with Travelpayouts (Aviasales, Hotellook) and
Trip.com. Adding Booking.com would let us serve accommodation on North American
routes, where our flight-alert volume is concentrated and where our current
hotel coverage is weakest.

Content: we publish route-level fare pages backed by our own multi-month price
history — data most metasearch engines discard within days — and produce short-
form video summarizing notable fare movements.
